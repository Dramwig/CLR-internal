\section{Conclusion}
Typed questions with calibrated answers give a practical way to read latent thoughts and latent messages: they outperform probes on option-conditioned questions, match a much larger verbalizer, transfer across models through small adapters, and turn KV-message auditing into a set of answerable sub-questions. The same experiments delimit what reading can do: a reader that knows which problems are hard does not know which latent continuation is better, and a reader that can see what a message says cannot stop perturbations that act through what it does not see.
