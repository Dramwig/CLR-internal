\begin{abstract}
Latent reasoning (continuous chain-of-thought) and latent communication (agents exchanging key--value caches) make models faster but leave their intermediate computation unreadable. Existing readers either output free text that cannot be calibrated, or a single scalar that answers one fixed question. We propose the \emph{Calibrated Latent Reader} (\clr{}), which reads a latent object from a source model and answers \emph{typed} questions---multiple choice, yes/no, or score---with temperature-calibrated probabilities and Mondrian split-conformal prediction sets. Labels come only from the source model's behavior or from program-generated task structure, never from hypotheses about what the latents ``should'' encode. On two latent-reasoning models (CODI-GPT-2 and CODI-LLaMA-1B) \clr{} beats MLP and attention probes by 4--15 points on questions that require interaction between the latent state and the answer options, gives smaller conformal sets than these probes, matches a 1.7B-parameter LoRA verbalizer at about 1/500 of its inference FLOPs, and transfers to a new source model by training only a small adapter. Applied to KV-cache messages between Qwen3 agents, typed auditing detects counterfactual message substitution at 93--98\% recall with 5\% false-positive rate, where norm-based and text-based audits are at chance. We also report two negative findings: readers that rank \emph{problems} well (AUROC 0.85) barely rank \emph{sibling} latent continuations (Spearman $\rho\approx0.1$), so reader-guided latent search does not beat greedy decoding; and semantic readability does not imply robustness, since small white-box KV perturbations change receiver behavior while evading every auditor we tried.
\end{abstract}
